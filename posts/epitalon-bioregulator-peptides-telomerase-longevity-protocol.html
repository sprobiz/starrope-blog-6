2. Epitalon Molecular Mechanism: TERT Activation & Telomere Lengthening

Telomeres—the protective ribonucleoprotein caps consisting of repeated TTAGGG tandem sequences at the ends of linear chromosomes—progressively shorten with every cycle of somatic cell division. When telomeres reach a critically short threshold (the Hayflick limit), cells enter irreversible senescence or undergo apoptotic cell death.

Detailed Molecular Cascades of Epitalon-Induced Telomerase Activation:

1. Binding to Histone Proteins & Chromatin Unwinding: Epitalon directly interacts with histone H1 and H3 proteins surrounding the promoter region of the hTERT (human Telomerase Reverse Transcriptase) gene. This binding alters nucleosome conformation, inducing local chromatin remodeling that exposes the TERT gene to RNA polymerase II.

2. Telomerase Enzyme Reconstitution: By upregulating hTERT gene expression, Epitalon stimulates the assembly of functional telomerase complexes (comprising the TERT catalytic protein subunit and the TERC RNA template). Telomerase synthesizes new TTAGGG hexanucleotide repeats onto chromosome ends, restoring telomeric structural integrity.

3. Somatic Stem Cell Rejuvenation: Telomerase reactivation mediated by Epitalon re-energizes resident adult stem cell niches (mesenchymal stem cells, neural progenitor cells, hematopoietic stem cells), restoring their proliferative capacity and tissue regeneration potential without causing neoplastic transformation.
